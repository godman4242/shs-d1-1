## Feel               (pacing, camera, animation weight, FX; if combat: rough per-encounter durations in seconds)

- Traversal pacing is brisk: each field/room screen is crossed in roughly 3-6 seconds of walking before hitting the next transition trigger, giving the ruins run a steady forward-momentum feel rather than exploration-heavy backtracking.
- Camera is a fixed side-on 2D plane per room, no vertical scroll — consistent with earlier segments; parallax background layers (distant mountains, drifting cloud/wind-streak decals) sell wind/weather atmosphere in the field and ruins-courtyard rooms specifically.
- Combat encounters are short: roughly 2-3 seconds of active hitstop/combo animation per kill (3-hit combos), immediately followed by a ~1-second "YOU WIN" full-screen freeze-frame flash, then a snap-cut back to the overworld HUD — no lingering victory pose or loot-drop animation on the field itself; loot is presented separately via the "YOU RECEIVED" parchment popup for at least one encounter.
- The "YOU WIN" screen uses a hard color-slam transition (instant cut to solid red field) rather than a fade, giving combat resolution a punchy, almost comic-book "impact panel" feel consistent with the game's over-the-top pulp tone.
- Menu interactions (PLAYER/STATS/SKILLS) are static full-screen overlays with no animated transitions observed between tabs — snappy, utilitarian UI feel, prioritizing information density (multiple bars/numbers) over presentation flourish.
- Stat-point spending has no distinct spend-animation/FX beyond the number ticking up and the "AVAILABLE STAT POINTS" counter decrementing — a purely numeric, low-ceremony upgrade interaction.
- The bottom-left mini portrait's small red "+" badge (appearing once stat/skill points are available) functions as a persistent "you have unspent points" nag, visible even out in the field, nudging the player back to the menu — a light retention/engagement hook.
