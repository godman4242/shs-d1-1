## Feel               (pacing, camera, animation weight, FX; if combat: rough per-encounter durations in seconds)

- Victory-to-dialogue transition is instant and static: no camera pan, just a straight cut from combat frame to a full red "YOU WIN" card, held roughly 3–6 seconds before the NPC dialogue portrait slides/cuts in.
- The dialogue-portrait screen keeps the "YOU WIN" text and red backdrop visible behind Satoshi's portrait — a cheap/efficient reuse of the win-screen background rather than a fresh backdrop, worth noting for our own recreation (same asset can serve both purposes).
- Inventory management in this window is notably fussy/menu-heavy — the player opens the PLAYER panel at least 3 separate times across ~25 seconds of footage, repeatedly comparing item weight and equip stats, dropping low-value junk (Branch, spare Garment/Straw Hat copies) to stay under the 100-weight cap. This reads as a real inventory-friction pain point of the original game (Tetris-style forced curation), not a deliberate "feel" choice — useful signal that our recreation should probably raise the cap or offer bulk-sell to avoid replicating this friction unless 1:1 fidelity is the goal.
- The chest search itself is anticlimactic on screen — no distinct "chest open" animation frame was captured (only "still closed with SEARCH CHEST prompt" then "player standing at ajar chest with coins already incremented"), suggesting the open/reward animation is brief (well under 2s, i.e. faster than our 0.4fps sampling).
- The forest ambush that follows has punchier pacing than the earlier boss fight: enemies keep flanking from both sides (left and right of frame), hit-combo counters climb quickly (up to 11), and screen flashes to a red vignette/tint briefly on player-taking-damage frames (t=57:32-ish and t=58:35, 58:52) — reconfirms the established "red flash = player hit" damage-feedback convention from other segments.
- Overall this ~2-minute window is mostly menu/UI-dwell time (roughly 35 of the ~120 seconds sampled are inventory screens) bookending two short skirmishes — a "victory lap" pacing beat between two combat setpieces.
